2. Why Risk Increases

Human mortality generally follows the Gompertz-Makeham Law. It states that a person's risk of death has two components:

Age-Independent Mortality:

Risks like accidents, disasters, or random infections that can occur at any age.

Age-Dependent Mortality:

The exponential increase in mortality risk due to biological aging, typically doubling every 8 years.

Interestingly, if you survive to an older age, your "Total Life Expectancy" actually increases. This is because you have successfully navigated the probabilistic risks of your younger years.

4. Understanding the Sex Gap

Across nearly all cohorts, females tend to outlive males. Biological factors include:

  • The "Jogging Heart" Effect: Estrogen helps clear "bad" cholesterol and provides cardiovascular protection until menopause.
  • The Double X Advantage: Having two X chromosomes allows for a backup of vital genes if one carries a mutation.
  • Behavioral Risk: Statistically, males are more likely to engage in high-risk behaviors and occupational hazards.
