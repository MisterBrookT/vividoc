Visualizing the Unseen

A Guide to Dimensionality Reduction for High-Dimensional Vectors

1. The Curse of Dimensionality

In modern Data Science, "High-Dimensional" refers to data with many features. For example, a single 28x28 pixel image has 784 dimensions. Humans can only perceive up to 3 dimensions. How do we understand the structure of data hiding in 700+ dimensions?

Dimensionality reduction is the process of mapping high-dimensional data X ∈ ℝⁿ to a lower-dimensional space Y ∈ ℝ² (or ℝ³) while preserving as much meaningful structure as possible.

2. Comparing Popular Algorithms

Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

Linear reduction. It rotates the data to find directions (principal components) that maximize variance. Fast, but misses complex non-linear patterns.

t-SNE / UMAP

Non-linear manifold learning. These focus on keeping "neighbors" together. Great for finding clusters, but distances between clusters might not be meaningful.

4. Key Considerations

  • Information Loss: You cannot squash data without losing something. In PCA, you lose the variance of the discarded components.
  • Global vs. Local Structure: Does the map preserve the "big picture" (PCA) or just the "immediate clusters" (t-SNE)?
  • Interpretability: Lower dimensions are often just mathematical abstractions; they don't always correspond to physical features like "height" or "weight".
