Stock-Flow Consistent (SFC) Modeling

The Rigorous Accounting Foundation of Macroeconomics

1. What is SFC Modeling?

Stock-Flow Consistent (SFC) modeling is an approach to macroeconomics that ensures all financial flows are accounted for and linked to changes in stocks (wealth). Created largely by Wynne Godley, it follows the principle of double-entry bookkeeping: every inflow to one sector must be an outflow from another, and every financial asset is someone else's liability.

The Golden Rule: "There are no black holes." Total financial savings across all sectors (Households, Firms, Government, Foreign) must sum to exactly zero.

4. Key Behavioral Equations

Behind the simulation are specific equations that govern how sectors behave:

  • Income: Y = C + G (GDP is the sum of consumption and government spending)
  • Disposable Income: YD = Y - T (What's left after taxes)
  • Consumption Function: C = α₁·YD + α₂·H₀ (Consumption depends on current income and past wealth)
  • Wealth Accumulation: ΔH = YD - C (Savings increase the stock of wealth)
