Oscillators & Harmonics

An Interactive Guide to Additive Synthesis

1. Properties of a Wave

Sound is a vibration traveling through a medium. In digital synthesis, we represent these as periodic waveforms. The three fundamental properties of any periodic wave are:

  • Frequency: How fast the wave oscillates (measured in Hertz), perceived as Pitch.
  • Amplitude: The "height" of the wave, perceived as Loudness.
  • Timbre: The "flavor" or character of the sound, determined by its Waveform Shape.

2. Standard Waveforms

Most synthesizers start with basic geometric shapes. Each shape has a unique harmonic profile:

Sine Wave: The fundamental building block. It contains no overtones, only a single frequency.

3. Additive Synthesis

According to Fourier's Theorem, any complex periodic waveform can be created by adding together multiple sine waves of different frequencies and amplitudes. These additional waves are called Harmonics.
