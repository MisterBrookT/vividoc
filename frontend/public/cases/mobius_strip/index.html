What is a Möbius Strip?

Non-Orientable Surfaces and Topological Manifolds.

In the rigid world of Euclidean geometry, every surface possesses absolute coordinates. Topology shatters this rule by caring only about continuous deformation. The Möbius strip introduces a radical twist—a bizarre, non-orientable surface where the concepts of "inside" and "outside" collapse into a singular continuous path.
$$ x(u,v) = \left( R + v\cos\frac{u}{2} \right)\cos u, \quad z(u,v) = \left( R + v\cos\frac{u}{2} \right)\sin u $$
  • u: Longitudinal angular progression spanning $[0, 2\pi]$.
  • v: Transverse span controlling the twisted width bandwidth.
  • R: The macroscopic radius generating the non-euclidean loop.
