01 — The Construction

De Casteljau's Algorithm

A Bézier curve of degree n is defined by n+1 control points \(P_0, P_1, \ldots, P_n\). The curve itself is never drawn by connecting these points — instead, a recursive linear-interpolation process called De Casteljau's algorithm computes every point on the curve.
For parameter \(t \in [0,1]\), define recursively: \[P_i^{(0)} = P_i \qquad P_i^{(r)} = (1-t)\,P_i^{(r-1)} + t\,P_{i+1}^{(r-1)}\] The point on the curve at parameter \(t\) is \(P_0^{(n)}\).

02 — Polynomial Form

Bernstein Basis

De Casteljau's algorithm is equivalent to evaluating the Bernstein polynomial representation of the curve. Each control point contributes a weighted "bump" function that is always non-negative and sums to 1 — guaranteeing the curve stays within the convex hull of the control points.
\[B(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} \binom{n}{i}(1-t)^{n-i}\,t^i\; P_i, \qquad t\in[0,1]\] where \(\displaystyle b_{i,n}(t) = \binom{n}{i}(1-t)^{n-i}t^i\) are the Bernstein basis polynomials.
The chart below plots the Bernstein basis functions for the current degree. Notice how each \(b_{i,n}(t)\) peaks at \(t = i/n\), meaning control point \(P_i\) has maximum influence at that parameter value. This gives designers intuitive control.

03 — C¹ Continuity

Joining Curves Smoothly

Single cubic segments are expressive but limited. Complex shapes require multiple segments joined together. G¹ continuity (visual smoothness) requires the end tangent of one segment to be collinear with the start tangent of the next. C¹ continuity is stronger: not just direction, but also magnitude must match.
For two cubics \(B_1\) and \(B_2\) joined at \(t=1\) and \(t=0\):
\(\text{G}^1:\; P_3^{(1)},\; P_0^{(2)},\; P_1^{(2)}\) are collinear
\(\text{C}^1:\; P_3^{(1)} - P_2^{(1)} = P_1^{(2)} - P_0^{(2)}\)
The interactive below enforces C¹: dragging either orange handle automatically mirrors its counterpart through the junction point. Toggle the constraint to see what happens without it.
Why it matters: Font outlines (TrueType, PostScript), vector graphics, and motion paths all rely on Bézier continuity. Adobe Illustrator's "smooth" anchor is exactly a G¹ constraint. Smooth animations require C¹ or higher to avoid velocity discontinuities.
