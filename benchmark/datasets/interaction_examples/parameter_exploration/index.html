What is the Lorenz Attractor?

Chaos Theory and the Butterfly Effect in Deterministic Nonlinear Systems.

At the heart of chaos theory lies profound sensitivity. Even the most rigorous, deterministic systems can become wildly unpredictable. A microscopic breeze from a butterfly's wing compounds exponentially over time, forcing atmospheric equations to trace out strange, infinitely complex topological attractors instead of stable orbits.
$$ \frac{dx}{dt} = \sigma(y - x), \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = x(\rho - z) - y, \quad \frac{dz}{dt} = xy - \beta z $$
  • σ (Sigma): The Prandtl number governing momentum vs. thermal diffusivity.
  • ρ (Rho): The Rayleigh number determining heat transfer mechanics.
  • β (Beta): The physical aspect ratio of convective rolls.
